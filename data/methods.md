# Therapy approaches offered

Cerenity practitioners work in five main modalities. The matching team picks one based on what you
described in intake, and your practitioner may combine them. None of them is better than the others
in general, they are better at different things.

## CBT, cognitive behavioural therapy

The most structured of the five. You learn to notice the thoughts that run underneath a feeling,
test whether they hold up, and change the behaviour that keeps them alive. Sessions usually come
with between-session practice. Strong evidence base for anxiety, panic and depression. Typically 12
to 20 sessions. Practitioners: Nadia Bekele, Iris Lindqvist.

## ACT, acceptance and commitment therapy

Instead of arguing with difficult thoughts, ACT teaches you to hold them more loosely and act on
what you actually value anyway. Good fit for burnout, chronic stress and long-running problems that
have resisted a fixing approach. Usually 8 to 16 sessions. Practitioner: Tomás Vieira.

## EMDR, eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing

A trauma-specific protocol. You recall a distressing memory while following a repeated left-right
stimulus, which appears to loosen the memory's emotional charge over repeated passes. Used for PTSD
and single-incident trauma. Requires a stabilisation phase first, so expect 3 to 4 preparatory
sessions before any reprocessing starts. Practitioner: Hana Sato-Mercer.

## IFS, internal family systems

Treats the mind as a set of parts that each formed to protect you from something. You learn to
approach the critical or frightened parts with curiosity rather than trying to silence them. Often
used for grief, shame and life transitions. Open-ended, commonly 20 or more sessions. Practitioner:
Daniel Okafor.

## EFT, emotionally focused therapy

Designed for couples and families. The focus is the pattern between people rather than either
person individually: what each partner does when they feel unreachable, and how that triggers the
other. Usually 15 to 20 sessions with both partners present. Practitioner: Priya Raghunathan.

## Choosing or changing approach

If an approach is not working for you, say so in session. Practitioners are expected to review the
plan at session six and ask directly whether it is helping. Changing modality does not require
changing practitioner unless the new approach is outside what they are trained in, in which case
the matching team handles it as a normal switch.
